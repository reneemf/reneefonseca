I develop statistical methods that make genetic predictions more accurate across groups, populations, and datasets — integrating contexts like sex, age, and treatment status, and modeling population structure to keep it from distorting results. In the lab

Genetic predictions are trained in one setting and applied in another — a different sex, a different age group, a different population, a different biobank — and they lose accuracy at every one of those boundaries. I build methods that make predictions hold up better across those moves, and think about where more representative data is the only real fix.

Methods

PGSC: polygenic score method integrating locus-specific gene–context interactions

Standard polygenic scores assume a variant affects a trait the same way in everyone — it doesn't always. PGSC models genetic effects that vary with sex, age, and treatment status, improving prediction across 48 traits in the UK Biobank and replicating in an independent cohort at Mount Sinai. Code is available here.

Population structure and portability

Capturing signals of population structure within a dataset so their effects can be removed from GWAS and polygenic scores — one route to scores that transfer better between biobanks.

Phenotype scale in G×E tests

Contributed source code and analysis to work led by Manuela Costantino showing that gene–environment signals can appear or vanish depending on how a trait is measured. A caution for anyone reading interaction results, including my own (see how we accounted for scale here).

Populations

Glaucoma in an Afro-Colombian cohort

A Fulbright year at Universidad del Valle in Cali, working to identify genetic variants linked to glaucoma risk and treatment response in a population almost entirely absent from existing genetic studies. Concurrently, I helped organize a seminar series bringing genomic medicine findings to the wider public.

Genetic and oral histories in Southwest India

Whole-genome analysis of four communities in Southwest India, alongside oral histories collected from community members — with the two treated as distinct kinds of knowledge rather than one confirming the other. I contributed a genetic health analysis and worked on returning results to the participating communities. (paper)

Widening the applicant pipeline
